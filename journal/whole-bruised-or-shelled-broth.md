# Whole, Bruised, or Shelled: A Side-by-Side Broth Extraction Trial
- URL: https://dhuneri.github.io/journal/whole-bruised-or-shelled-broth/
- Published: 2026-11-14T17:10:00.000Z
- Updated: 2026-11-14T17:10:00.000Z
- Description: Three matched vegetable broths, one unspiced control, and a practical way to compare smoke, cooling aroma and bitterness.
- Author: Risita Sedain
- Tags: Recipes

## The question in the pot

A whole black cardamom pod is an appliance as well as an ingredient. Its shell holds the seeds together, exposes a limited surface to the stock, and makes retrieval easy. Break it and that geometry changes. Remove the shell and the comparison changes again: now less plant material enters the pot, even if the seeds came from a pod of the same starting weight.

There is a useful test here, but not a universal winner waiting to be announced. Nepalese large black cardamom is usually identified as *Amomum subulatum*; it is not interchangeable with every spice sold as black cardamom. Different lots can carry different amounts of smoke from processing, and the language a taster chooses—smoky, resinous, cooling or bitter—does not measure an individual chemical. [\[1\]](#ref-1)

## Recipe at a glance

Yield: four 400 ml test batches, each enough for several tasting spoonfuls. Active work: about 30 minutes, plus broth preparation. Equipment: four small identical covered pans, fine scale readable to 0.01 g, fine sieve, four removable fine-mesh spice bags, tasting cups, timer and thermometer if available.

For the common base: 2 litres drinking water; 300 g carrot, 200 g onion, 150 g celery or another mild vegetable, and 6 g fine salt. For the treatments: three comparable intact, clean large-cardamom portions of 1.50 g each, preferably from the same lot. A 0 g fourth portion is the unspiced control. If a pod does not weigh exactly 1.50 g, use carefully matched pieces and log the actual mass instead of pretending otherwise.

## Method | Base, portions and infusion

1. Rinse and roughly chop the vegetables. Bring them and the water to a gentle simmer in a covered pot; simmer 35 minutes. Strain through the same sieve into a clean vessel, add the salt and stir. Cool briefly and measure. If needed, add hot drinking water to bring the base to 1.6 litres; divide into four equal 400 ml portions. This is a mild base designed for comparison, not a claim about traditional stock.
2. Prepare three 1.50 g parent-pod portions. Leave one intact. Lightly bruise the second just until its shell cracks; retain all fragments. Open the third, separate its seeds and shell, and put only its seeds into its bag. Weigh and record both seed and discarded shell masses; this treatment does not have the same total mass as the other two. Use the same empty bag in the control.
3. Put one portion and its bag in each pan. Bring each to the same gentle simmer, cover, and hold for 20 minutes. Avoid vigorous boiling. Remove the bags at the same elapsed time, press none of them, and stir. Top each batch back to 400 ml with hot water if evaporation differed; record any correction. Serve matched 30 ml samples at the same comfortably warm temperature. Reserve a second sample from each for tasting after a further five minutes, without continuing extraction.

## What a tasting can—and cannot—show

Label cups with random three-digit codes and vary their serving order between tasters. Do not reveal which preparation is which. Provide water, plain crackers and small portions; anyone uncomfortable with a sample can stop. Score aroma intensity, smoky impression, cooling/resinous impression, bitterness and overall preference separately from 0 to 5\. An intensity score is not a quality verdict. Record the actual words a taster uses rather than placing a flavor in their mouth in advance. [\[2\]](#ref-2)

A sample sheet can use one row per taster and code, with separate fields for each score, serving temperature and comments. Repeat on another day with fresh base and the same cardamom lot if the result matters. One household session produces observations, not proof that all shelled seed behaves a particular way.

Interpret the comparisons carefully. Whole versus bruised tests a change in physical preparation at similar parent mass, although individual pods still vary. Shelled versus whole combines exposure with the removal of husk material, so it does not isolate seed exposure alone. A follow-up that adds a separately weighed husk-only treatment can clarify the shell’s contribution. A proper replicated design is needed before presenting a general ranking.

## Troubleshooting and service

If all samples taste like vegetables, either the pod lot is mild, the infusion too short, or the base too assertive. Do not quietly change just one pan. Log the result and repeat all treatments with one altered variable. If a sample becomes harsh, stop at 20 minutes and try a shorter time in a new matched run; do not call harshness proof that a specific terpene or smoke compound caused it.

Discard bags and any hard pod fragments before serving. Use food-safe utensils and chill leftovers promptly in shallow containers; follow local food-safety guidance. Never leave the broth warm overnight. The point is not to make four pots that confirm a theory. It is to make four pots that allow the theory to be wrong. [\[3\]](#ref-3)

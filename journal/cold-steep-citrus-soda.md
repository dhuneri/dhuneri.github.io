# A Kitchen Soda Base: Cold-Steeped Black Cardamom & Citrus Syrup

- Description: A measured refrigerated steep, three extraction times, and a dilution ratio built for adjustment.

## A cold drink with a strong ingredient

Black cardamom does not have to be a pot of rice or a long-cooked stew. Its resinous, sometimes smoky character can give a citrus soda a savory edge. The practical question is not whether it belongs in a glass, but how little is needed before it overwhelms the citrus. We have not tasted these combinations; the quantities below are proposed starting points for a kitchen trial.

The word cold matters. Here it means steeping in a refrigerator, not leaving a jar on a counter. It does not mean the result must be gentler or cleaner than a hot infusion: that would require matched samples and tasting.

## A measured base for three steep times

Yield: about 600 g total syrup before straining losses, divided across three test jars. Equipment: digital scale, three clean lidded jars, fine sieve, timer and a refrigerator at 4°C or below. Ingredients: 300 g granulated sugar; 300 g drinking water; 1.50 g whole black cardamom, divided into three 0.50 g portions; fresh lemon or lime juice and chilled soda water for serving. Use black cardamom identified as *Amomum subulatum*, not essential oil. [\[1\]](#ref-1)

Heat the water just enough to dissolve the sugar while stirring; do not reduce it by prolonged boiling. Cool promptly, then refrigerate until at 4°C or below. Split by weight into three clean jars, 200 g per jar. Lightly bruise each 0.50 g spice portion in the same way and put one in each jar. Keep the containers covered and refrigerated throughout.

## The time experiment

Steep for 2, 6 and 12 hours, respectively. To finish together, add spice to the 12-hour jar first, the 6-hour jar six hours later and the 2-hour jar ten hours later. Label each jar with start time and treatment; use identical jars and syrup temperature. At the common end point strain each through the same clean fine sieve without squeezing and weigh the recovered syrup. For safety and clarity, use the finished syrup immediately that day; keep it cold while serving and discard leftovers rather than claiming a tested shelf life.

Serve 10 g syrup diluted with 40 ml chilled water for the comparison. Code samples and randomize order; score spice intensity, smoky impression, bitterness, perceived sweetness and preference. An unspiced syrup control helps separate the spice from the sugar. The 2-, 6- and 12-hour labels are planned treatments, not findings. Their sensory difference, if any, must be recorded. [\[2\]](#ref-2)

## Build one highball

In a tall glass with clean ice, combine 25 g strained syrup and 15 ml fresh lemon or lime juice. Add 100 ml cold unflavored soda water, stir gently once and serve. The starting ratio is 1 part syrup by mass to 4 parts soda by volume; it is a kitchen convention, not a strict dimensionless mass ratio. Add citrus separately for every drink. A zest twist is optional, but wash the fruit and avoid the bitter white pith.

Calculation: 200 g of syrup per jar, before straining losses, could supply up to eight drinks at 25 g each; three jars could theoretically supply 24\. Actual yield will be lower after sampling and straining, so weigh what remains. At the 25 g serving dose, a 10 g comparison sample would reduce availability; plan the panel before inviting a crowd.

## Adjust one variable at a time

If the drink tastes thin, reduce soda to 80 ml before adding more syrup; this changes dilution, not the infusion. If it is too sweet, try more lemon juice in a separate glass, not in the stored batch. If smoke seems excessive, compare a shorter steep or smaller pod dose in a fresh matched run. Do not try to rescue a jar by masking it with a large quantity of juice and then report that the spice was balanced.

Sugar concentration alone does not establish the safety of a homemade diluted infusion. Use clean equipment, cover the syrup, keep it refrigerated and make only what can be served after the trial. No room-temperature storage, home canning or botulism-safety claim is implied. Refrigeration slows microbial growth but does not make every product indefinitely safe. [\[3\]](#ref-3)

This is a useful soda when the citrus remains recognizably citrus and the cardamom earns its space. Whether the two do that at 2, 6 or 12 hours belongs in the tasting notes—not in a conclusion written before the glass exists.

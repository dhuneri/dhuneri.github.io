# Smoked Pods Meets 70% Cacao: An Honest Pairing Log
- URL: https://dhuneri.github.io/journal/black-cardamom-cacao-ganache/
- Published: 2026-11-14T17:25:00.000Z
- Updated: 2026-11-14T17:25:00.000Z
- Description: A controlled seed-in-cream ganache trial, with a plain control and no invented tasting results.
- Author: Risita Sedain
- Tags: Recipes

## Start with the chocolate, not the story

Dark chocolate already brings bitterness, sweetness, fat and its own roasted notes. Black cardamom can add something interesting to that mix—or make it harder to taste the chocolate. Both outcomes are plausible. Neither is a result until someone makes the ganache and records what happens.

This recipe is an untested experimental starting point, not an account of a completed kitchen session. Use food-grade Nepalese large black cardamom, *Amomum subulatum*, and a 70% cacao chocolate whose label and brand are recorded. The percentage tells you the proportion of cacao ingredients, not the chocolate’s entire sensory profile.

## The four-bowl setup

For each bowl: 80 g finely chopped 70% chocolate and 40 g pasteurized heavy cream. Make four bowls: one without spice; three with 0.15 g, 0.35 g and 0.70 g black cardamom seeds, respectively, removed from their shells. Total for four bowls: 320 g chocolate, at least 160 g cream plus a small extra amount for topping up, and 1.20 g seeds. A 0.01 g scale is useful. Record the pod lot and actual seed weights.

The chocolate-to-cream ratio is 2:1 by mass, a common starting point for firmer dark-chocolate ganache, not a guarantee of texture for every chocolate and cream. A scientific review describes ganache as a complex emulsion and emphasizes that formulation and processing affect its stability and shelf life. [\[1\]](#ref-1)

## Method | A short toast, a measured infusion

1. Chop chocolate evenly and divide into four identical heatproof bowls. Set out four small pans or work in clean, labeled batches. Coarsely crack each seed dose with the same pressure; do not grind to powder.
2. For each spiced batch, warm its seeds in a dry pan over low heat for approximately 30–60 seconds, moving them continuously. Stop if they darken rapidly or smell burnt; if they burn, discard that treatment and start again. Transfer promptly to a clean pan with slightly more than 40 g cream. Treat the control cream for the same time without seeds. The toast time is an initial procedure, not an established optimum.
3. Warm cream gently until steaming and just beginning to simmer at the edge. Remove from heat, cover and steep for 10 minutes. Strain through a fine sieve without pressing. Weigh the infused cream; top each portion up with fresh cream to exactly 40 g, then rewarm each gently to steaming before adding to chocolate. This keeps the final cream dose constant, although topping up can dilute the infusion by different amounts; record top-up mass.
4. Pour each 40 g portion onto its 80 g chocolate, wait one minute and stir slowly from the center until smooth. If unmelted chocolate remains, warm the bowl briefly over barely warm water, keeping water out. Cover and refrigerate promptly. Allow all samples the same chilling time; return only the sample portions to a similar cool serving temperature for tasting.

## The log that has not yet been filled

Assign random three-digit codes and serve small equal portions, with water or plain crackers between them. Ask tasters separately about smoke, woody or resinous aroma, chocolate clarity, bitterness, texture and preference on a 0–5 scale. Include an open comment field: if someone says “pine needles,” write it down; do not supply the phrase before the tasting. Record panel size, chocolate brand, cream fat, dose, steep time, serving temperature and preparation date. [\[2\]](#ref-2)

Define a useful decision before tasting: for example, choose the dose that leaves chocolate recognizable while achieving the desired spice intensity for a tart filling. If the preferred sample is the unspiced control, that is a legitimate outcome. Repeat on another day before making claims about repeatability.

## If the emulsion fails

An oily or grainy ganache can result from formulation or temperature differences; it should not automatically be blamed on cardamom. Record the appearance and compare with the control. Small amounts of warmed cream may help bring a split mixture together, but record any addition because it changes the ratio. If a sample burns, develops an off odor or has been handled unsafely, discard it rather than using it for sensory testing.

This is a fresh dairy preparation, not shelf-stable confectionery. Use pasteurized cream, clean utensils and refrigerate at 4°C or below promptly. Make for immediate use; consume within two days as a conservative household practice and do not infer a validated shelf life. Shelf life depends on formulation, water activity, hygiene and storage. Allergens include milk; check chocolate labels for additional allergens. [\[1, 3\]](#ref-1)

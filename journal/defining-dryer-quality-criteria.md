# Defining the Quality Criteria: What Must a Modern Cardamom Dryer Actually Accomplish?
- URL: https://dhuneri.github.io/journal/defining-dryer-quality-criteria/
- Published: 2026-11-13T14:30:00.000Z
- Updated: 2026-11-13T14:30:00.000Z
- Description: Essential-oil retention, uniform moisture reduction, contaminant control, affordability and reliability—translated into tests rather than promises.
- Author: Risita Sedain
- Tags: Research, Farmers

## The specification starts at the receiving end

The easiest dryer to admire is an empty one: clean trays, a tidy furnace and a thermometer holding its number. The meaningful test comes later, when a real harvest fills the machine, the weather changes, a joint leaks, and someone has to decide whether the batch is ready to sell. A dryer is not an achievement because its drawings are modern. It is an achievement if the people who load, fire, monitor and maintain it can obtain an acceptable product, repeatedly, for a cost and workload they accept.

Large cardamom (*Amomum subulatum*) is sold into different markets, including markets that value a smoky profile and buyers seeking a cleaner, less smoky capsule. A single universal idea of “better aroma” cannot settle that difference. Before comparing equipment, write the intended product specification with its buyer and the operators. This article is a research-based procurement framework, not a record of a new field trial. [\[1, 2\]](#ref-1)

## 1\. Capacity: match the harvest, not a brochure

Specify fresh capsule mass per batch, loading depth, number of trays and practical throughput per 24 hours. Record how a load is weighed and whether stalks are included. A nominal 300 kg machine is not useful if the normal harvest is 120 kg and the machine cannot be fired economically at partial load—or if loading and unloading require a crew that is not available.

A Sikkim field study published in 1996 reported a roughly 300 kg fresh-capsule natural-convection dryer reaching approximately 10% moisture in 24 hours, compared with 55 hours for the conventional *bhatti* and 42 hours for a curing-house dryer in that comparison. These were different designs and loading conditions; the figures are not a guaranteed result for a Nepal installation. The paper is evidence that a design can be tested, not a transferable warranty. [\[2\]](#ref-2)

## 2\. Moisture: measure the lot’s variation

Agree on final moisture as a percentage on a stated basis, the analytical method, sample preparation, cooling period, and the number and positions of samples. A single composite average can conceal wet pockets: take separate samples from different tray levels, edges and centers, and retain their individual results. Set a maximum permissible spread and decide what happens when a sample fails: further drying, segregation or rejection.

Measure water activity separately when the buyer’s storage and safety plan requires it; it is not interchangeable with moisture. The European Spice Association recommends a water-activity target of at most 0.65 for herbs and spices in its business-to-business quality guidance. That is a voluntary industry target within the document’s stated scope, not a universal legal limit or a guarantee that pathogens or existing toxins have disappeared. Agree the target and sampling plan for this product and market. [\[3\]](#ref-3)

## 3\. Aroma: preserve a product, not just an oil percentage

Volatile-oil yield is an indicator, not a complete description of flavor. Specify botanical identity, capsule or seed, dry- or wet-mass basis, extraction method and analytical laboratory. If reporting a relative retention percentage, define the comparison sample and the denominator: dried product at the end versus matched starting material, corrected to comparable dry matter where appropriate. Distillation yield alone does not establish the sensory profile of the finished spice.

For a comparison, split fresh harvest material into matched lots and replicate each dryer treatment on different runs. Blind-code the finished samples and ask trained assessors to record smoke, resin, off-notes and overall suitability against the buyer’s desired style. Measure oil and selected volatile compounds alongside sensory judgments. A chromatographic peak cannot tell you whether a cook likes the result, but a tasting panel cannot measure contaminant ppm.

## 4\. Contamination: replace “zero PAHs” with an auditable limit

Indirect heating separates combustion gas from product air in principle; it does not prove that a finished spice contains zero polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). Leaks in a heat exchanger, contaminated fuel, dirty ambient air, grease or handling can introduce contaminants into an otherwise indirect system. Do not claim “PAH-free” or “completely smoke-free” on the basis of a mechanical drawing.

Name the target PAH analytes, representative lot sampling procedure, accredited laboratory, method, limit of quantification and intended buyer specification or regulatory destination. The Codex Alimentarius Code of Practice for the Reduction of PAH Contamination provides recognized international guidance on fuels, design and indirect heating. In laboratory reports, state non-detects clearly with their numerical limits; never report “zero.” [\[4\]](#ref-4)

## 5\. Economics and the person at the furnace

Request the installed price, transport, foundation, chimney, wiring, training, spares and maintenance. For electricity, determine the supply voltage, required breaker rating, peak power draw, energy per batch, and whether three-phase power or a utility transformer upgrade is needed. For biomass, record fuel species, moisture condition, mass consumed per dry kilogram and who gathers or pays for it.

On each run measure active fire-tending hours, time spent gathering and carrying fuel, loading, tray rotation, cleaning, unloading and bagging. Unpaid family labor is still labor. If a new machine requires hourly stoking through the night, write that into the comparison—do not hide it in an efficiency formula.

## 6\. Reliability and safety: demonstrate the failure case

Record available power, maximum load, interruptions, fuel quality and spare-parts lead time. Ask what happens when the grid drops mid-batch: does the crop spoil, can the machine switch to biomass or natural convection, or does it hold heat safely until power returns? Specify fire risks, flue-gas safety, hot-surface contact, carbon monoxide risks and emergency shutdown.

A sensible acceptance test includes at least three representative loads, including a partial load if harvests are staggered, monitored from loading to bagging with real harvest material and the farm’s normal operators. If the machine cannot complete three runs without an engineer from Kathmandu on site, it has not passed.

## A commissioning decision, not a claim

The minimum useful outcome is a signed set of results: what was loaded, what emerged, how the operators felt about the work, what the buyer paid, and what the dryer cost to build and run. If a manufacturer will not sign that protocol, their confidence belongs in a sales presentation, not on a farm.

The final specification belongs to the people who must use and pay for the dryer, together with the customers who buy from them. Everything else is a trial.

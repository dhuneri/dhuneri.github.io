# The Threshold Test: Exactly How Much Black Cardamom Ruin a Pot of Rice?
- URL: https://dhuneri.github.io/journal/threshold-test-basmati-rice/
- Published: 2026-11-14T17:15:00.000Z
- Updated: 2026-11-14T17:15:00.000Z
- Description: A controlled four-dose trial—0.5 g, 1.5 g, 3.0 g and 5.0 g—plus an unspiced comparison.
- Author: Risita Sedain
- Tags: Recipes

## A number needs a definition

A pod can lift a pot of basmati or crowd everything else off the plate. “Too much” is a useful kitchen judgment, but not a property that appears at one exact gram weight for every person. Pod age, drying method, exposure time, rice brand, accompanying dishes and a taster’s tolerance all matter.

The proposed experiment compares four doses of the same batch of *Amomum subulatum* with unspiced rice. It does not claim the experiment has been performed, and it cannot identify a universal threshold. It gives a cook a way to find a threshold for this rice, this pod lot and this panel.

## The five pots

Per pot: 300 g dry basmati rice, 450 ml drinking water and 4.5 g fine salt. This is approximately four portions per pot, or twenty portions for the full experiment; halve all five pots consistently if cookware permits. Use five similar lidded pans. Use whole-pod material from one lot and a scale readable to 0.01 g; do not substitute a count of pods for a weight.

Treatments: 0 g control, then 0.50 g, 1.50 g, 3.00 g and 5.00 g of whole pods or large pod pieces. Lightly bruise each dose to the same degree and place in identical food-safe mesh bags. Keep the shell and seeds together. All four doses are experimental quantities, not recommendations or proven taste rankings.

## Calculation callout | Spice per serving

Each pot contains four intended servings. Divide each dose by four: 0 g = 0 mg per serving; 0.50 g = 125 mg; 1.50 g = 375 mg; 3.00 g = 750 mg; 5.00 g = 1,250 mg. These are the masses put into the pot divided by four—not the amount of cardamom compounds absorbed by a serving. The bag and pods are removed, so actual extraction and ingestion are unknown.

For comparison per 100 g dry rice, the four experimental doses are 0.17 g, 0.50 g, 1.00 g and 1.67 g respectively, rounded to two decimal places. Do not compare those numbers to doses from a different preparation as if the flavor response were linear.

## Method | Cook alike, taste blind

1. Rinse each weighed rice portion the same number of times and drain for the same period. Put rice, measured water, salt and one spice bag in each pan. The control receives an empty bag. Use equal-sized burners or cook the pans in a consistent sequence while recording start and end times.
2. Bring each pan to a simmer, cover, reduce heat to low and cook approximately 12 minutes, until the water is absorbed; follow the rice packet if it specifies a different reliable method, applying it to every pot. Turn off heat and rest covered for 10 minutes. Remove the bag, fluff with a clean fork and record any differences in sticking or water left. If one pot burns, exclude that pot and rerun it rather than attributing burnt flavor to cardamom.
3. Serve the same weight, for example 25 g, from each pot at a similar warm temperature in randomly coded cups. Change serving order across tasters. Start with the control for an optional orientation sample, then taste the coded cups without identifying the treatment. Plain water and a break between samples help; anyone may stop before a strong dose. [\[1\]](#ref-1)

## What does “ruin” mean?

Before tasting, agree a rule such as: a sample is unacceptable if a taster scores overall acceptability below 3 on a 0–5 scale. Record overall acceptability, cardamom intensity, perceived bitterness and willingness to eat a full portion separately. This rule is a study choice, not a scientific definition of ruin. Also record whether tasters expect the rice to be served plain or with a strongly flavored curry: the intended meal changes the judgment.

For each person, report the lowest tested dose judged unacceptable, if any. If none is unacceptable, report “not reached at 5.0 g”; if 0.5 g is already unacceptable, report “at or below 0.5 g.” The true personal boundary may lie between tested doses; do not invent a precise value. With several people, present the proportion rejecting each dose and the small panel size, not a population estimate.

## Rice deserves the same care as the spice

Serve promptly. If keeping leftovers, divide into shallow portions and cool as quickly as possible, ideally within one hour, then refrigerate. UK Food Standards Agency advice is to keep cooked rice refrigerated for no more than one day, reheat only once and serve steaming hot throughout. Reheating does not undo every toxin formed during improper storage. [\[2\]](#ref-2)

A useful result could be that the same person wants 0.5 g in plain rice and more beside a heavy stew. That is not an experimental failure. It is a reminder that “the right amount” belongs to a dish and its diners, not to a slogan.

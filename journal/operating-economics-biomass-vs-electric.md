## A bill is not a tariff

Before choosing a drying system, a grower needs to know what one saleable kilogram costs to produce—and whether the batch will finish if the power cuts out. An electric dryer promises control, cleaner air and less overnight fire-tending; a biomass *bhatti* runs on local wood and requires no grid transformer. But comparing them fairly requires putting every cost on the table: active stoking hours, firewood collection, electricity tariffs, grid reliability and moisture loss.

The example below is a deliberately hypothetical engineering and cost model. It is not a recorded Taplejung drying run, an endorsement of any machine or a financial guarantee. It illustrates how to structure a transparent comparison before committing capital.

## Step 1: put fresh and dry kilograms on the same basis

Assume a 120 kg fresh batch at 80% moisture, wet basis, dried to 10% moisture, wet basis. Assume no dry-solids loss. Initial dry matter is 120 × (1 − 0.80) = 24 kg. Final total batch mass is 24 ÷ (1 − 0.10) = 26.67 kg. Water removed is 120 − 26.67 = 93.33 kg.

The implied fresh-to-dry ratio is 4.5:1\. A regional postharvest review cites roughly 4:1 to 5:1 depending on material and curing method. Record initial and final mass for every real batch; do not calculate energy from nominal capacity alone. [\[3\]](#ref-3)

## Step 2: distinguish heat from a meter reading

If the latent heat requirement is approximated as 2.4 MJ per kilogram of water removed, 93.33 × 2.4 ≈ 224 MJ of theoretical heat is needed, before accounting for sensible heating of capsules, air exchange, structural thermal losses or exhaust humidity. The energy purchased is always substantially larger than the theoretical minimum.

A resistance heater, a heat pump and a biomass furnace do not convert purchased energy to useful drying heat with equal efficiency. In a Sikkim field comparison, a modified natural-convection flue dryer consumed roughly 0.85 kg of firewood per kilogram of fresh capsule; a conventional bhatti consumed roughly 1.77 kg/kg fresh in that study. Design, insulation and operation matter far more than the fuel category alone. [\[4\]](#ref-4)

## Step 3: the illustrative batch ledger

For the same 26.67 kg modeled dry output, assume an electric dryer consumes 110 kWh and needs four active operator hours. Assume an improved biomass dryer consumes 100 kg of firewood and needs eight active hours across the cycle. Assume an illustrative tariff of NPR 12/kWh, an assumed biomass opportunity cost of NPR 8/kg, and an assumed labor rate of NPR 150/hour.

The electric batch is 110 × 12 + 4 × 150 = NPR 1,920, or NPR 72.00/kg dry. Biomass is 100 × 8 + 8 × 150 = NPR 2,000, or NPR 75.00/kg dry. On these assumptions, operating costs are close. A change in tariff, firewood price or labor immediately alters the balance.

## Step 4: test the assumption that moves the decision

Hold the 110 kWh, four labor hours and output constant. At assumed tariffs of NPR 8, 12 and 16/kWh, the electric operating cost is NPR 55.50, NPR 72.00 and NPR 88.50 per dry kilogram respectively. For biomass, holding eight labor hours constant, firewood at NPR 4, 8 and 12/kg yields NPR 60.00, NPR 75.00 and NPR 90.00/kg.

If wood is gathered at low cash cost from own-farm pruning and family labor is unpriced, biomass appears far cheaper in cash terms. If firewood is purchased and transported over steep trails, or if labor is hired at harvest peaks, biomass can easily exceed electricity costs.

## What the ledger does not include

- **Capital and finance:** Add the installed purchase price, foundation, shelter, chimney, three-phase extension and spare parts amortized over expected lifespan.
- **Reliability:** A grid failure during peak curing can ruin an entire 120 kg load if the dryer has no passive convective venting or biomass fallback. Verify local feeder uptime. [\[1, 2\]](#ref-1)
- **Quality premia:** If an indirect or electric dryer consistently commands an auditable price premium from specialty buyers due to lower PAHs or intact color, that margin must offset the capital cost.
- **Labor nature:** Active handling hours differ from elapsed hours. An electric dryer that runs unattended frees daytime fieldwork; an unautomated biomass bhatti demands watchful overnight tending.

## Measure the next batch before buying the next machine

For each system, weigh the input and output, measure initial and final moisture on the same basis, record the actual meter reading or fuel bundles burned, and log every operator hour. The answer may be electric, biomass, shared village infrastructure or no new investment. The calculation earns trust when it is based on your real meter, your real scales and your real ledger.

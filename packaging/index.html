DHUNERI · The packaging

A pack with purpose.

More than a container, DHUNERI’s packaging carries the story of Nepal’s black cardamom — its land, its people and its lasting value.

The thought behind the pack

Not just a wrapper. A story worth carrying.

The packaging brings together the DHUNERI identity, Nepal’s Himalayan landscape and the character of black cardamom. It is designed to help people recognise the product while making room for the information and people behind it.

The pack is a visual introduction to origin, craft and product identity. Any farmer, quality, certification or traceability information must be supported by verified records before publication or print.

The design, in five parts

Five details. One considered pack.

Each element has a purpose. Together, they make the packaging more recognisable, informative and connected to the product’s journey.

01

Brand identity

The DHUNERI wordmark, Nepali black cardamom name, Himalayan landscape and friendly character create a recognisable front identity.

Shown in approved artwork
02

Farmer story

A dedicated panel can introduce the growers and communities behind the spice. Names, portraits and stories should be included only with consent and accurate attribution.

Content to be verified
03

Batch traceability

A batch reference and QR destination are planned to connect each pack with its growing area, harvest and processing information.

Concept · not live
04

Quality information

Product grade, net weight, storage guidance and quality checks belong on the pack once the actual product specifications and supporting records are confirmed.

Specifications to be confirmed
05

Share-back commitment

A clear explanation of how value is returned to growers can make the relationship visible. It should describe an agreed, documented model—not an unverified promise.

Proposed · not a verified claim

Packaging formats

One identity, three expressions.

The approved visual direction is adapted across a pouch, tin and jar. Each format keeps the same DHUNERI identity while responding to a different container shape.

Pouch

Primary concept

The flexible pack concept for the core product presentation.

Tin

Format concept

A more structured container concept using the same label identity.

Jar

Format concept

A display and storage concept that keeps the product visible.

Important: These are visual format studies. Container materials, dimensions, capacities, closures and commercial availability are not confirmed.

02 / GROWING REGIONS

Four districts,
one story.

Black cardamom cultivation is concentrated along the high mist slopes of eastern Nepal, across four primary districts whose steep valleys and rainfall create Nepal’s premier high-altitude cardamom terroir.

01 1,400–2,100 m

Taplejung

Foothills of Kanchenjunga

Steep north-facing slopes with cold nights and heavy mist. Taplejung produces dense, slow-ripening capsules renowned for deep resinous complexity.

Tamor & Kabeli river basins
02 1,000–1,800 m

Panchthar

Mid-hill forest clearings

Nutrient-rich forest loam sheltered by expansive alder canopies. The rolling ridges provide consistent autumn breezes ideal for natural pre-curing.

Hewa & Yangnam ravines
03 900–1,600 m

Ilam

Historic mist slopes

The historic heart of commercial cultivation in Nepal. Persistent blanket fog corridors nurture lush vegetative growth and vibrant capsule development.

Mai Khola catchment
04 1,200–2,000 m

Sankhuwasabha

Arun Valley gorges

Carved by the deep Arun River gorge, these steep, dramatic microclimates offer high humidity paired with extreme natural hillside water drainage.

Num & Arun corridor

These represent historical cardamom-growing regions across eastern Nepal—not a map of exclusive DHUNERI commercial supply.

PLATE 02 / PHENOLOGY

The growing
year.

Cardamom farming is intimately bound to the monsoon cycle and the mountain seasons. From spring pollination on the forest floor to late autumn curing, every stage demands careful timing.

Documentary photograph
Active growth phase · June to August

Monsoon: Growth & Moisture

Between June and August, dense cloud cover blankets the hills. Torrential monsoon rains nourish the leafy shoots while steep hill slopes allow water to flow freely past the roots without ponding.

PLATE 03 / LOCAL MEASURE

A hill measures
itself in ropani.

In Nepal’s eastern hills, land is rarely described in hectares or acres. It is measured in ropani—a historic unit shaped by the natural contours of mountain ridges.

Most cardamom groves are small, family-tended plots nestled along ravines. Understanding the ropani is understanding the human scale of mountain cultivation.

508.7 Square metres
16 Aana per ropani
≈19.7 Ropani per hectare
